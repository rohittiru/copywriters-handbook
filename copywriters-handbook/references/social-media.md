# Writing for Social Media

Social media writing follows platform-specific conventions. What works on LinkedIn differs from Facebook, which differs from Twitter/X. But the fundamentals of clear, benefit-driven, concise writing apply everywhere.

## Universal Social Media Rules

1. **Be concise.** Avoid extra words. Posts should be short and direct, starting with an interesting hook or lead.
2. **Write to engage.** Deliver interesting, valuable information within 2–3 sentences or short paragraphs.
3. **Encourage interaction.** End posts by asking for comments: "What do you think?" / "Do you agree?" / "What's your experience?"
4. **Use a business page**, not a personal profile, for marketing. A personal page for business looks amateur (unless your name IS the brand).
5. **Save long content for blogs.** Social media readers prefer quick reads. Link to longer content elsewhere.

## Platform-Specific Approach

**LinkedIn:**
- Professional, informative tone
- Share industry insights, thought leadership, case studies
- Longer posts (up to ~1,300 characters) perform well
- Lead with a hook in the first 2 lines (before the "see more" fold)
- Use line breaks for readability
- Carousel documents and native video get strong reach
- Comment engagement matters — reply to every comment

**Facebook:**
- More casual, conversational tone
- Mix of educational content, stories, and offers
- Visual content (images, video) dramatically outperforms text-only
- Boosted posts extend reach beyond your followers (paid)
- Avoid controversial topics (politics, religion) on business pages
- FB's algorithm favors posts that generate conversation

**Twitter/X:**
- Extremely concise — every word counts
- Use threads for longer stories or arguments
- Strong opening line is critical (it's the only "headline" you get)
- Hashtags for discoverability (1–2 per tweet, max)
- Reply to others' tweets to build visibility

**Instagram:**
- Visual-first platform — the image/video IS the headline
- Captions can be long but front-load the key message
- Use Stories for behind-the-scenes, polls, and quick engagement
- Hashtags are essential for discovery (up to 30, but 5–10 targeted ones work better)
- Reels get the broadest reach

## The Funnel Approach

Use different titles and angles across platforms, but funnel all readers to the same destination (blog post, landing page, or opt-in).

Example — all three posts link to the same blog, but use different hooks:
- LinkedIn: Industry insight angle with data
- Facebook: Casual, curiosity-driven angle
- Twitter: Punchy one-liner with link

**Funnel flow:** Social post → Blog/Content → Email opt-in → Landing page → Sale

## Content Calendar Strategy
- Post on a regular schedule (e.g., Tuesday and Friday)
- Create themed content series readers expect and look forward to
- Mix content types: educational tips, stories, client results, behind-the-scenes, direct offers
- Ratio guideline: 80% value/education, 20% promotion

## Engagement Rules
- Be personable — write as if speaking to one individual
- Participate in conversations and respond to comments
- Never dismiss someone's source without counter-evidence
- State opinions as opinions, not facts
- Don't try to argue someone out of a belief they didn't arrive at through logic
- Avoid profanity and extreme language — it alienates more than it attracts

## Social Media Ad Copy
When writing paid social ads, the fundamentals of headline writing and the motivating sequence still apply. Lead with a benefit, arouse curiosity, be specific, and include a clear CTA. Platform ad guidelines (Facebook, Google, etc.) impose compliance restrictions — review them before writing. Creative workarounds exist for restricted categories (e.g., health claims → food-based angles instead of direct claims).
