# Writing for Video

Video is one of four learning modes (watching, listening, reading, doing). Produce content in multiple formats to reach all audiences — but video is uniquely powerful because it combines visual demonstration with spoken persuasion.

## Video Types
- **Short-form** (under 2 minutes): social media, pre-roll ads, product demos
- **Long-form** (5–45+ minutes): video sales letters (VSLs), webinars, training, tutorials
- **Testimonial videos**: customer stories filmed professionally with signed releases
- **Explainer videos**: animated or live-action product/service overviews

## Video Sales Letters (VSLs)
A VSL is essentially a long-form sales letter read aloud over slides or simple visuals. Scripts can run 3,000–6,500+ words. Structure follows the motivating sequence:

1. **Hook** (first 15–20 seconds) — if you don't grab them here, they're gone
2. **Problem agitation** — make the viewer feel the pain
3. **Story/credibility** — establish why you're the authority
4. **Solution introduction** — position your product as the answer
5. **Features and benefits** — detailed walkthrough
6. **Proof** — testimonials, case studies, data, demonstrations
7. **Offer** — what they get, bonuses, pricing
8. **Guarantee** — remove risk
9. **CTA** — tell them exactly what to do
10. **Urgency/scarcity** — reason to act now

## Short-Form Video Tips
- Open with the most compelling visual or statement — no slow intros
- Get to the point in the first 3 seconds (social media auto-play means silent viewing)
- Add captions/subtitles — most social video is watched without sound
- End with a clear CTA
- Keep it under 60 seconds for social; under 15 seconds for pre-roll ads
- One message per video — don't try to cover everything

## Script Writing Principles
- Write for the ear, not the eye — use conversational language
- Short sentences, simple words (even more important than in print)
- Read the script aloud — if it sounds awkward spoken, rewrite it
- Use transitions that signal what's coming: "Here's the thing...", "Now, here's where it gets interesting..."
- Vary pacing — alternate between information-dense and emotional/story sections
- Direct the viewer's attention: "Look at this...", "Notice how..."

## Production Notes for Copywriters
- The script is the foundation — bad scripts produce bad videos regardless of production quality
- Simple production often outperforms expensive production (authenticity > polish)
- Provide the videographer/editor with clear notes on b-roll, graphics, and text overlays
- For testimonials: let customers speak in their own words, keep it natural and unscripted
- Always get signed video releases from anyone who appears on camera

## 6 Video Script Types

### 1. The 60-Second TV Advertisement
Classic commercial format. Two-column script: VIDEO (left) describes visuals, AUDIO (right) describes dialogue/voiceover/music. See `references/tv-radio.md` for the 12 commercial formats.

### 2. Explainer Video Script
Shows how a product or service works. Typically uses a mix of on-screen text, live footage, and animation. Focus on one specific problem and one solution. Best for homepage videos and product pages.

### 3. Interviewing Video Script
Structured interview with pre-planned questions given to the interviewee in advance. Use a teleprompter placed behind camera level so the interviewee can reference answers while still appearing to speak naturally to the audience.

### 4. Corporate Video Script
Executive-led video covering company performance, milestones, or vision. Intersperse the speaker with charts, graphs, and b-roll to maintain visual interest. Works for annual reports, investor updates, and internal communications.

### 5. Training Video Script
Onboarding, software tutorials, best practices, company culture. Must be clear, step-by-step, and assume zero prior knowledge. Can be longer than marketing videos.

### 6. Online Video Script (Web/Social)
Average homepage video: 2–3 minutes. B2B videos average ~4 minutes. Half of viewers watch in entirety. Second-level pages (product demos, applications) can go 5–7 minutes.

**"One presentation, one topic."** Each video should cover only one product, one idea, or one offer. Attention wanes quickly online.

## Script Length Guide
120 words per minute of narration. A 2-minute video = 240 words. A 10-minute video = 1,200 words (~5 double-spaced pages).

## The Creative Brief (Before Writing the Script)
Before writing, gather from the client:
- All available product/service information
- Any existing storyboards or visual concepts
- Target audience definition
- The single objective of the video
- Where the video will be shown (website, social, trade show, sales meeting)
- Desired length
- Budget constraints (affects production complexity)
- Existing brand guidelines (tone, colors, logos)
