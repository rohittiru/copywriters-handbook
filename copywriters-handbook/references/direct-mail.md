# Writing Direct Mail

Direct mail has 5 seconds to grab attention before it goes in the trash. Every element must work together to get the reader to open, read, and respond.

## The Direct Mail Package
1. **Outer envelope** — teaser copy, return address, urgency elements
2. **Sales letter** — the most important element; does the heavy selling
3. **Brochure/flyer** — visual support, product details, features/benefits
4. **Reply device** — order form, reply card, or business reply envelope
5. **Lift note** — a second, shorter letter (often from a different person) that adds urgency or credibility

## Outer Envelope
The teaser's only job is to get the envelope opened. Approaches:
- **Benefit teaser:** "Inside: how to cut your heating bill by 50%"
- **Curiosity:** "What do Japanese managers have that American managers sometimes lack?"
- **Official/plain:** No teaser; looks like personal or business correspondence
- **Urgent:** "Time-sensitive material — please open immediately"
- **Free offer:** "Your FREE gift certificate enclosed"

## The Sales Letter

The letter is the workhorse. Structure follows the motivating sequence:

**Opening (the lead):** Hook with the strongest benefit, a provocative question, a story, or a bold promise. The lead must compel the reader to keep going. Never open with warm-up or throat-clearing.

Types of letter leads:
- **Direct promise:** State the biggest benefit immediately
- **Problem-agitation:** Describe the reader's pain, then offer relief
- **Story:** Begin with a narrative that illustrates the product's value
- **Question:** Ask something that resonates with the reader's situation
- **News:** Announce something new, important, or time-sensitive
- **Quotation/testimonial:** Open with a powerful customer quote

**Body:** Features/benefits, proof, testimonials, details about the offer. Follow the motivating sequence. Use you-orientation throughout.

**Close:** Restate the offer, emphasize the guarantee, create urgency, and tell them exactly how to respond. Include a P.S. — it's the third most-read element after the headline and first paragraph. Use the P.S. to restate the main benefit, add a bonus, or create urgency.

## Letter Formatting
- Use a headline or Johnson Box (text in a bordered box above the salutation)
- Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences max)
- Indent paragraphs or add space between them
- Underline or bold key phrases (sparingly)
- Use handwritten-style annotations in margins for a personal touch
- Sign in blue ink (or blue font) for contrast against black text
- Multiple pages are fine — long letters often outperform short ones for complex offers

## Reply Device
Make it easy to respond:
- Pre-fill the customer's name and address
- Use check boxes for options
- Restate the offer and guarantee on the reply card
- Accept multiple payment methods
- Include a toll-free number and website as response alternatives

## Testing in Direct Mail
Test one variable at a time: list, offer, format, copy. The mailing list is typically the single biggest factor in response rates — even great copy can't sell to the wrong list.
