## ALL 15 SALES LETTER LEAD TYPES

### 1. State the Offer
"Yours FREE for 10 days — the legendary music of Beethoven — Nine Symphonies that epitomize the beauty and harmony denied him in life. Plus FREE Preview bonus."
*Use when: The offer itself is so attractive it IS the selling point.*

### 2. Highlight Free Literature
"Dear Friend: We have reserved for you a free copy of Prentice Hall's TAX-SAVING STRATEGIES."
*Use when: Generating inquiries with a free resource. Center the pitch on the benefits of the FREE resource, not the product.*

### 3. Make an Announcement
"In the world of U.S. stamps, there is only one name older than that of the Federal Bureau of Engraving and Printing itself... Now, for the first time ever..."
*Use when: You have genuine news — a new product, a first, or a one-of-a-kind event.*

### 4. Tell a Story
"Dear Executive: Three years ago this month, a man I know — he was then a vice president of a big corporation in Illinois — walked into his boss's office and handed in his resignation... But then, about a year ago, the picture began to change..."
*Use when: You want empathy. Stories hold attention and relate to readers' own lives.*

### 5. Flatter the Reader
"Dear Mr. McCoy: One of a kind. Is that phrase a little trite? I used to think so until I tried to find you. Now I know what 'one of a kind' really means. The process of finding your name and address was the advertising equivalent of panning for gold..."
Also: "Dear Entrepreneur: You're it! You're the kind of person free enterprise is built on."
*Use when: Selling luxury, exclusive, or premium products. Mirror the reader's self-image.*

### 6. Write Peer-to-Peer
"Dear Writer: I don't have the great American novel in me. I flunked Poetry 102 in college. My first, last, and only short story was rejected by 14 magazines..."
*Use when: Writing to a special-interest group. "I am like you. I know your problems."*

### 7. Personal Message from the President
"Personal Message to Microcomputer Owners from Joseph W. Tamargo, President of FUTURESOFT..."
Also: "A memo recently crossed my desk that said I would have to RAISE MY PRICES — NOW — to offset our spiraling operating costs! But I said, 'NO! NOT YET!'"
*Use when: The owner's personal stake adds credibility.*

### 8. Use a Provocative Quote
Open with a surprising statistic, controversial opinion, or startling fact in quotation marks.
*Use when: You have a genuinely surprising statement that hooks curiosity.*

### 9. Ask a Question
"Dear Friend, What do you think when you see a letter that starts with 'Dear Friend'?"
*Use when: The question resonates with the reader's concerns. Never ask a question they'd answer "no" or "I don't care" to.*

### 10. Make It Personal
"As you may already know, we have been doing some work for people who have the same last name as you do. Finally, after months of work, THE AMAZING STORY OF THE BLYS IN AMERICA is ready for print and you are in it!"
*Use when: You can insert the reader's name or personal details into the pitch itself.*

### 11. Identify the Reader's Problem
"You know how difficult it is to look your best if dental problems are causing discomfort and pain..."
Also: "If you can't afford higher taxes. If you're afraid to walk the streets at night. If you're sick and tired of corrupt government officials..."
*Use when: Your product solves a specific, painful problem. Start with the pain.*

### 12. Stress a Benefit
"Just published ... HOW TO MAKE YOUR ADVERTISING MAKE MONEY."
*Use when: The benefit is so strong it speaks for itself. "Don't tell your prospect about your grass seed, tell him about his lawn."*

### 13. Use Human Interest
"When I was 16, my father died of a heart attack..."
*Use when: A personal story connects emotionally to the product.*

### 14. Let the Reader In on Inside Information
"Here's a fresh-from-the-printer reprint of our latest WALL STREET JOURNAL ad... We're sending you our new ad months before it will break in the press."
*Use when: You can make the reader feel they're getting exclusive, early access.*

### 15. Sweepstakes
"American Family Publishers Announces America's First By-Mail GUARANTEED MULTIMILLION-DOLLAR SWEEPSTAKES OFFER."
Three tiers: "You have won" > "You may have won" > "You may win"

## BODY COPY TECHNIQUE EXAMPLES

### Features to Benefits (The Pencil Exercise)
| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Wooden cylinder | Resharpened as often as you like for clear writing |
| Hexagonal shape | Won't roll off your desk |
| Rubber eraser on end | Correct mistakes cleanly and quickly |
| Tight-fitting metal band | Holds eraser snugly; no ruined pencils |
| 7.5 inches long | Long writing life |
| 0.25 inch diameter | Slender, comfortable to hold and write with |
| No. 2 graphite | Writes smoothly yet crisply |
| Yellow exterior | Bright, stands out in a pencil holder |
| Sold by the dozen | One trip gives months of supply |
| Available in gross | Lower cost per unit for offices/schools |
| Made in U.S.A. | Quality product; strengthens the economy |

### You-Oriented Rewrites
**Before:** "Bank Plan is the state-of-the-art in user-friendly, sophisticated financial software..."
**After:** "Bank Plan can help you balance your books. Manage your cash flow. And keep track of customers who haven't paid their bills."

**Before:** "The objective of the daily cash accumulation fund is to seek the maximum current income..."
**After:** "The cash fund gives you the maximum return on your investment dollar with the lowest risk. And you can take out as much money as you like — whenever you like."

**Before:** "To cancel an order, return the merchandise to us in its original container..."
**After:** "If you're not satisfied, simply return it and tear up your invoice. You won't owe us a cent. What could be fairer than that?"

### Sentence Shortening Rewrites
**Before:** "Today every penny of profit counts and Gorman-Rupp wants your pumps to work for all they're worth."
**After:** "Today every penny of profit counts. And Gorman-Rupp wants your pumps to work for all they're worth."

**Before:** "Over thirty thousand aerospace engineers are members now. To join them, send your check..."
**After:** "Over thirty thousand aerospace engineers are members now. Join them."

**Before:** "...helps you stabilize teeth quickly, easily, and economically."
**After:** "...helps you stabilize teeth. Quickly. Easily. And economically."

### Sentence Fragments
"Basic Eye Emphasizer does it all. It's the one eye makeup everyone needs. The only one."
"It doesn't take much to block the door to success. A flash of an idea that slips your mind. A note that never gets written."

### Conjunctions as Sentence Starters
"The first lesson is free. But I can't call you. You have to take the first step."
"The choice is simple. Be a pencil pusher. Or get the Messenger. And move ahead at the speed of sound."

### One-Sentence Paragraph
"For many ad agency people, industrial advertising is a difficult chore. It's detailed work, and highly technical. To write the copy, you need someone with the technical know-how of an engineer and the communications skills of a copywriter.

That's where I can help."

### Conversational Style (IBM)
"There's a world of information just waiting for you. But to use it, study it, enjoy it and profit from it, you first have to get at it. Yet the facts can literally be right at your fingertips."

### Concise Substitutions
free gift → gift | whether or not → whether | he is a man who → he | most unique → unique | comes to a complete stop → stops | exhibits the ability to → can | on an annual basis → yearly | features too numerous to mention → many features | can be considered to be → is | simple and easy to use → easy to use | RAM memory → RAM

### Big Word → Simple Word
obtain → get | operate → run, use | optimum → best | parameters → factors | prioritize → rank | purchase → buy | substantiate → prove | utilize → use | terminate → end | visage → face

## PERSUASION EXAMPLES

### The CPA Ad (Full Motivating Sequence)
**Headline:** "WOULD YOU PAY $1,000 TO SAVE $5,500?"
**Body:** "Last year, a local flower shop decided to hire an accountant... You can imagine how delighted they were when they hired an accountant who showed them how they could pay thousands less in income tax... I am their accountant, and I'd like to tell you how..."

### Secondary Promise
**Big:** "$2 stock could be worth $100"
**Secondary:** "But even if the treatment is a total failure, the stock could still earn early-stage investors a 500% gain within 24 months."

### False Logic
- Harry & David pears: "Not one person in 1,000 has ever tasted them" (sounds exclusive, not unpopular)
- Newsletter vs. money manager: $99 vs. $2,000 comparison
- American Speaker vs. speechwriter: $99 vs. $5,000 comparison
- McDonald's "Billions Sold": popularity implies quality
- Metals broker: "95% shipped from stock" (the supplier's stock, not theirs)

### USP Examples
- M&M's: "Melts in your mouth, not in your hand"
- Geico: "15 minutes could save you 15%"
- Dollar Shave Club: "Shaving and Grooming Made Simple"
- Blackmer Pump: "There are only certain times you should call Blackmer. Know when?"
- Beer purity: All bottles washed in live steam — no competitor had claimed it

### BDF → Headline
IT professionals: Beliefs (smarter than others), Desires (appreciation), Feelings (adversarial with users) → "Important news for any IT professional who has ever felt like telling an end user, 'Go to hell.'" (Most successful letter ever sent.)

### Cooking oil insight from focus group
User said: "I fried chicken in the oil and then poured the oil back into a measuring cup. All the oil was there except one teaspoon." → Became basis of successful TV campaign: food doesn't absorb the oil.

### Diabetic needle insight
Users praised the needle because it was sharp. Non-users see "sharp" as negative. But sharper needles = easier, pain-free injections. → Successful campaign built on the claim of sharpness.

## ENVELOPE TEASERS (Worst to Best)
1. Worst: "THIS IS THE BEST WIDGET EVER MADE" (boasting)
2. Better: "THIS WIDGET MAY SAVE YOU UP TO $500" (benefit)
3. Best: "ENCLOSED IS YOUR FREE WIDGET" (reward for opening)

Strong: "Inside: The Secret to Living Longer and Feeling Better... Without Dieting or Special Exercise"
Weak: "Sawyer Life Insurance Announces Its 50th Year of Operation"
Rule: A weak teaser is worse than no teaser (plain envelope).

## HOW TO USE THIS SWIPE FILE
1. Identify the headline type that fits your product/audience
2. Study 3-5 examples of that type
3. Extract the structural pattern
4. Apply the pattern to your product's strongest benefit
5. Rate with the 4 U's — aim for 3+ on three dimensions
6. Write 6-8 variations; use rejected winners as subheads

## ADVERTISING IDEAS CHECKLIST (When You're Stuck)

When stuck for an approach, scan this list and match to your product:

- **Question ad** — asks a question in the headline, answers it in the body
- **Quiz ad** — reader takes a quiz to determine if they're a prospect
- **News ad** — announces something new about a product
- **Direct ad** — straightforward presentation of facts
- **Indirect ad** — obscure headline arouses curiosity, body copy reveals
- **Reward ad** — promises a reward for reading the ad
- **Command ad** — commands the reader to take action
- **Price-and-where-to-buy** — announces a sale, gives price and location
- **Reason-why ad** — presents reasons to buy
- **Letter ad** — written in letter form
- **Before-and-after** — shows improvements from using the product
- **Testimonial** — user or celebrity speaks in favor
- **Case history** — detailed product success story
- **Free information ad** — offers free brochure/booklet, focuses on getting the response rather than selling directly
- **Story ad** — tells a narrative involving people and the product
- **Reader identification** — headline selects the specific audience
- **Information ad** — gives useful info relating to the product's category, not pushing the product directly
- **Location ad** — product shown in unusual location to highlight versatility or ruggedness
- **Fictional characters** — ad centers around a character (Mr. Whipple, Green Giant)
- **Fictional places** — ad centers around a place (Marlboro Country)
- **Advertiser in ad** — company owner appears to speak about their own product
- **Invent a word** — create a term to describe the product ("athlete's foot" was invented by adman Obie Winters to sell horse liniment that cured ringworm; "halitosis" was popularized to sell Listerine)
