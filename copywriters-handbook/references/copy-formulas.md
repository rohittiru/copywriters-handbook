# Copy Structure Formulas: Selection Guide + Worked Examples

Four proven frameworks. Don't ask the user which one to use — **select it automatically** based on the product, audience, and format. This file tells you how.

---

## Auto-Selection Logic

**Step 1: Identify the ad type** (see `references/ad-type-taxonomy.md`)
**Step 2: Match to the right formula using this decision tree:**

```
Is the product complex or technical, and the buyer rational/analytical?
  YES → ACCA
  NO ↓

Is the copy selling an aspirational transformation or lifestyle outcome?
  YES → 4 P's
  NO ↓

Does the buyer not yet realize they have the problem your product solves?
  YES → Motivating Sequence
  NO ↓

Default → AIDA
```

### Quick-Match Table

| Product / Situation | Formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS landing page | **ACCA** | Buyer needs to understand what the software does before they want it |
| Weight loss program landing page | **4 P's** | Aspirational transformation — "picture your new body" |
| Tax accountant ad | **Motivating Sequence** | Buyer doesn't realize they're overpaying — need must be shown |
| E-commerce product page | **AIDA** | Simple product, clear benefit, emotional appeal |
| B2B enterprise software demo page | **ACCA** | Complex product, multiple stakeholders, rational decision |
| Online course sales page | **4 P's** | Sells a future version of yourself |
| Email subject line + body | **AIDA** (compressed) | Quick format, needs fast attention→action flow |
| Investment newsletter VSL | **4 P's** or **Motivating Sequence** | Transformation + hidden need |
| Dental practice mailer | **Motivating Sequence** | People tolerate dental problems; need must be surfaced |
| Fashion brand social ad | **AIDA** | Emotional, visual, impulse-driven |
| Industrial pump trade ad | **ACCA** or **Motivating Sequence** | Technical buyer, or need-revealing approach like Blackmer |
| Nonprofit fundraising letter | **4 P's** | Paint the picture of impact; promise change |
| Subscription box landing page | **AIDA** | Simple concept, clear benefit, emotional excitement |
| Consulting services homepage | **Motivating Sequence** | Must show the cost of NOT hiring you |
| GTM agency lead-gen page | **ACCA** or **Motivating Sequence** | B2B buyer needs comprehension, or doesn't realize the gap |

## Formula 1: AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)

The classic workhorse. Use when the product has clear emotional appeal and the buyer already understands the category.

### Structure
- **Attention:** Headline grabs with a benefit, news, or curiosity
- **Interest:** Opening creates interest by expanding the headline's promise
- **Desire:** Body builds desire through benefits, proof, emotional appeals
- **Action:** Close tells the reader exactly what to do

### Worked Example: E-Commerce Product (Bluetooth Speaker)

**A — Attention (Headline):**
"Room-Filling Sound That Fits in Your Pocket — Now 40% Off"

**I — Interest (Lead):**
"The NovaBoom Mini delivers the rich, deep bass you'd expect from a speaker three times its size. And right now, you can get it for less than the price of dinner for two."

**D — Desire (Body):**
"Imagine this: you're at a beach barbecue. Someone asks, 'Where's that music coming from?' You hold up something the size of a coffee cup. 12-hour battery life. Waterproof. Pairs in 3 seconds. Over 14,000 five-star reviews on Amazon."

**A — Action (CTA):**
"Add to cart now — sale ends Sunday at midnight."

## Formula 2: ACCA (Awareness → Comprehension → Conviction → Action)

The rational framework. Use when the buyer needs to UNDERSTAND before they can WANT — complex products, technical audiences, B2B.

### Structure
- **Awareness:** Make reader aware of the problem and that a solution exists
- **Comprehension:** Explain clearly what the product is and how it works — educate before selling
- **Conviction:** Build conviction through proof, evidence, and logical argument
- **Action:** Close with a clear next step

### Worked Example: SaaS Landing Page (Project Management Tool)

**Aw — Awareness:**
"Your team wastes 7.5 hours per week in status meetings, email chains, and hunting for the latest version of a file. That's almost a full workday — gone."

**Co — Comprehension:**
"FlowBoard replaces your meetings, email threads, and shared drives with one visual workspace. Every task, file, deadline, and conversation lives in a single board your whole team can see. Drag a card from 'In Progress' to 'Done' — and everyone knows instantly."

**Cv — Conviction:**
"In a 6-month pilot with 340 teams, FlowBoard users reduced meetings by 62% and shipped projects 3.2 weeks faster on average. Here's what the VP of Engineering at Dropbox said: [testimonial]. SOC 2 compliant. Integrates with Slack, Jira, and GitHub in under 5 minutes."

**A — Action:**
"Start your free 14-day trial — no credit card required."

## Formula 3: The 4 P's (Picture → Promise → Prove → Push)

The transformation framework. Use when you're selling a future version of the buyer's life — aspirational outcomes, lifestyle changes, "before and after."

### Structure
- **Picture:** Paint a vivid mental image of life AFTER the product
- **Promise:** Promise this picture will come true if they buy
- **Prove:** Prove it with testimonials, case studies, demonstrations
- **Push:** Push for immediate action with urgency or scarcity

### Worked Example: Online Course Sales Page (Copywriting Course)

**Pi — Picture:**
"Six months from now, you open your laptop to find three new client inquiries in your inbox. Your last project — a landing page for a SaaS startup — generated 4x their previous conversion rate. The founder just referred you to two friends. Your calendar is booked eight weeks out. You set your own rates. You work from anywhere."

**Pr — Promise:**
"The Copy Accelerator Program is the same system I used to go from writing $200 blog posts to $5,000 landing pages in under a year. Inside, you'll get the exact frameworks, templates, and client-getting strategies that built my six-figure freelance business — and you can start using them this week."

**Pv — Prove:**
"Here's what happened when 847 students went through the program: [3 specific testimonials with dollar amounts and timelines]. Average income increase: 340% within 12 months. Satisfaction rate: 94%."

**Pu — Push:**
"Enrollment closes Friday at midnight. Join now and get the bonus Client Acquisition Toolkit ($497 value) free. 60-day money-back guarantee — if you're not landing better clients within 60 days, you pay nothing."

## Formula 4: The Motivating Sequence (Bly's Preferred)

The need-revealing framework. Use when the audience doesn't realize they have the problem your product solves, or when the pain needs to be surfaced and agitated before the solution is introduced.

### Structure
1. **Get attention** — headline with the single strongest benefit
2. **Show a need** — make the reader FEEL the problem they didn't know they had
3. **Satisfy the need** — position your product as the solution
4. **Prove it** — testimonials, data, demonstrations, credentials
5. **Ask for action** — specific CTA with urgency

### Worked Example: CPA Firm Ad (from the book)

**1 — Attention:**
"WOULD YOU PAY $1,000 TO SAVE $5,500?"

**2 — Show a need:**
"Most small business owners do their own taxes. They don't realize they could be overpaying by thousands every year — simply because they don't know the latest deductions and shelters available to them."

**3 — Satisfy the need:**
"Last year, a local flower shop decided to hire an accountant. They worried about the fee, but realized they didn't have the time or expertise to do it themselves. They were delighted when I showed them how to pay thousands less than they originally thought they'd owe."

**4 — Prove it:**
"I am their accountant, and I've helped dozens of firms save $2,000... $3,500... even $5,500 a year or more by taking advantage of legitimate tax regulations."

**5 — Ask for action:**
"Call for a free 15-minute tax review. No obligation."

### Worked Example: GTM Consultancy Lead-Gen Page

**1 — Attention:**
"Why Your LinkedIn Strategy Is Generating Followers — But Not Pipeline"

**2 — Show a need:**
"You're posting consistently. Engagement looks decent. But when you check your CRM, the leads aren't there. The problem isn't your content — it's that content without conversion architecture is just broadcasting. You're building an audience, not a pipeline."

**3 — Satisfy the need:**
"Genning builds the system that turns your LinkedIn presence into a lead engine: targeted outreach sequences, conversion-optimized email campaigns, and content that's engineered to generate replies — not just likes."

**4 — Prove it:**
"In our first 90 days with [client], we generated 47 qualified inbound leads from a LinkedIn audience of just 3,200 connections. Here's what their CEO said: [testimonial]."

**5 — Ask for action:**
"Book a free 30-minute pipeline audit. We'll show you exactly where your leads are leaking — and how to fix it."

## Combining Formulas

For long-form copy (VSLs, long landing pages, sales letters), you can combine elements:
- Open with **4 P's** (paint the picture) → transition to **Motivating Sequence** (show the need, satisfy, prove) → close with **AIDA** urgency
- Use **ACCA** for the logical argument sections, then shift to **4 P's** for the emotional close

The formulas aren't rigid boxes — they're thinking tools that ensure your copy has the right structural bones.
