# Swipe File: Direct Mail

## All 15 Sales Letter Lead Types

### 1. State the Offer
"Yours FREE for 10 days — the legendary music of Beethoven — Nine Symphonies that epitomize the beauty and harmony denied him in life. Plus FREE Preview bonus."
*When: The offer is so attractive it IS the selling point.*

### 2. Highlight Free Literature
"Dear Friend: We have reserved for you a free copy of Prentice Hall's TAX-SAVING STRATEGIES, a helpful book for Corporate Executives."
*When: Generating inquiries. Center the pitch on the benefits of the FREE resource.*

### 3. Make an Announcement
"In the world of U.S. stamps, there is only one name older than that of the Federal Bureau of Engraving and Printing itself... Now, for the first time ever..."
*When: You have genuine news — a first, a new product, a one-of-a-kind event.*

### 4. Tell a Story
"Dear Executive: Three years ago this month, a man I know — he was then a vice president of a big corporation in Illinois — walked into his boss's office and handed in his resignation. Two weeks later, he started his own company... Almost from the start, the new company caught on. But then, about a year ago, the picture began to change..."
*When: You want empathy. The reader thinks "this could happen to me."*

### 5. Flatter the Reader
"Dear Mr. McCoy: One of a kind. Is that phrase a little trite? I used to think so until I tried to find you. Now I know what 'one of a kind' really means. The process of finding your name and address was the advertising equivalent of panning for gold..."
Also: "Dear Entrepreneur: You're it! You're the kind of person free enterprise is built on."
*When: Selling luxury, exclusive, or premium products.*

### 6. Write Peer-to-Peer
"Dear Writer: I don't have the great American novel in me. I flunked Poetry 102 in college. My first, last, and only short story was rejected by 14 magazines..."
*When: Writing to a special-interest group. "I am like you. I know your problems."*

### 7. Personal Message from the President
"Personal Message to Microcomputer Owners from Joseph W. Tamargo, President of FUTURESOFT... I want to tell you why I have chosen to send you an actual condensation of the Operating Instructions..."
Also: "A memo recently crossed my desk that said I would have to RAISE MY PRICES — NOW! But I said, 'NO! NOT YET!'"
*When: The owner's personal stake adds credibility and urgency.*

### 8. Use a Provocative Quote
Open with a surprising statistic, controversial opinion, or startling fact in quotation marks. Must raise a question or arouse curiosity.
*When: You have a genuinely surprising statement.*

### 9. Ask a Question
"Dear Friend, What do you think when you see a letter that starts with 'Dear Friend'?"
*When: Question resonates with reader's concerns. Never ask one they'd answer "no" to.*

### 10. Make It Personal
"As you may already know, we have been doing some work for people who have the same last name as you do. Finally, after months of work, THE AMAZING STORY OF THE BLYS IN AMERICA is ready for print and you are in it!"
*When: You can insert the reader's name or details into the pitch itself.*

### 11. Identify the Reader's Problem
"You know how difficult it is to look your best if dental problems are causing discomfort and pain..."
Also: "If you can't afford higher taxes. If you're afraid to walk the streets at night. If you're sick and tired of corrupt government officials..."
*When: Product solves a specific, painful problem. Start with the pain.*

### 12. Stress a Benefit
"Just published ... HOW TO MAKE YOUR ADVERTISING MAKE MONEY. A clear, concise guide to effective advertising..."
*When: Benefit is so strong it speaks for itself. "Don't tell about your grass seed, tell about his lawn."*

### 13. Use Human Interest
"When I was 16, my father died of a heart attack..."
*When: A personal story connects emotionally to the product.*

### 14. Let the Reader In on Inside Information
"Here's a fresh-from-the-printer reprint of our latest WALL STREET JOURNAL ad... We're sending you our new ad months before it will break in the press. We wanted you to be the first to know."
*When: Making the reader feel they're getting exclusive access.*

### 15. Sweepstakes
"American Family Publishers Announces America's First By-Mail GUARANTEED MULTIMILLION-DOLLAR SWEEPSTAKES OFFER."
Three tiers: "You have won" > "You may have won" > "You may win"

## Envelope Teaser Examples

**Three formats (worst to best):**
1. Worst — boasting: "THIS IS THE BEST WIDGET EVER MADE"
2. Better — benefit: "THIS WIDGET MAY SAVE YOU UP TO $500"
3. Best — reward inside: "ENCLOSED IS YOUR FREE WIDGET"

**Strong:** "Inside: The Secret to Living Longer and Feeling Better... Without Dieting or Special Exercise"
**Weak:** "Sawyer Life Insurance Announces Its 50th Year of Operation"

Rule: Use a teaser ONLY if it's irresistible. A weak teaser is worse than a plain envelope (which gets opened because it looks like personal mail).

## Salutation Examples
- "Dear Farmer" / "Dear Lawyer" / "Dear Computer Enthusiast" / "Dear Future Millionaire" — always better than "Dear Sir" or "Dear Friend"
- Johnson Box: headline centered in a bordered box above the salutation

## Response-Boosting Techniques
- Always include a response mechanism (reply card, reply envelope, toll-free number, URL)
- Use business reply envelopes (postage-paid = more responses)
- Order forms with tear-off stubs generate more response
- Offer a premium (gift) related to the product
- Allow a negative response: "Not interested now. Try again in ___"
- Use physical objects in the mailing — bulky envelopes almost always get opened
- Put a time limit on the offer
- Include a P.S. — it's the third most-read element (after headline and first paragraph)

## The Letter/Brochure Division of Labor
"The letter sells, the brochure tells." Every package needs a letter. Brochures are optional. Use a brochure when: product is visually impressive, requires demonstration, has detailed specs, or the letter is devoted entirely to benefits and needs a separate piece for features.
