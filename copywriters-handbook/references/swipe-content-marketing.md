# Swipe File: Content Marketing

## Historical Context
Content marketing isn't new. Claude Hopkins (1866-1932) offered free booklets to test print ad response. In the 1960s-70s, industrial companies produced detailed "design manuals" (like Koch Engineering's "tray manual" for refinery equipment) — spiral-bound, fold-out blueprints, costing several dollars each to produce. They called it "giving away free information." Today we call it "content marketing" and "lead magnets."

The terminology evolved: "free booklets" → "bait pieces" (because they "hooked" prospects into leads) → "lead magnets" (the free content magnetically attracts responses).

## The 4 Levels of Content (Lowest to Highest Value)
1. **Why** — tells them why they should do something ("Why You Need a Call Center")
2. **What** — tells them what to do ("7 Steps to Planning an In-House Call Center")
3. **How** — tells them how to do it (tips, techniques, step-by-step)
4. **Done for you** — does it for them (templates, tools, calculators, done-for-you services)

Most content stays at levels 1-2. The higher you climb, the more valuable and differentiated your content becomes.

## 7 Deadly Sins of Content Marketing
1. Not being a subject matter expert — writing about topics you don't deeply understand
2. Mediocre research — "Google goulash" cobbled from other articles, no originality or firsthand knowledge
3. Taking the easy way out — staying at "why" and "what" levels instead of climbing to "how" and "done for you"
4. Not promoting your content — creating it is only half the job
5. Not tracking results — if you can't measure it, you can't improve it
6. Not crediting sources — always provide full URLs for statistics and claims
7. Giving opinions without evidence — the best opinions are evidence-based

## Lead Magnet Examples
- White papers and special reports
- Free chapters or excerpts
- Design manuals and technical guides
- Checklists and templates
- Online calculators and tools
- Video tutorials and mini-courses
- Webinar recordings
- Industry survey results

## Content Headline Patterns
Use the same headline formulas from the main swipe file. Content headlines that work best:
- How-to: "How to Plan and Implement an In-House Call Center"
- Numbered list: "7 Steps to..." / "5 Mistakes That..."
- Question: "Is Your Call Center Costing More Than It Should?"
- News: "New Research Reveals..."

## Key Rule
"If your ad generated a profitable response despite weak content, imagine how much more money you could have made with strong content." Quality content is not optional — mediocre content adds to the cloud of "content pollution" growing on the web.
