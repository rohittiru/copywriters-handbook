# Ad Type Taxonomy: Identify What You're Writing Before You Write It

Not all ads are the same. Before writing a single word, identify WHICH TYPE of ad you're creating — because each type demands fundamentally different copy length, tone, content, and CTA strategy.

---

## The Four Primary Ad Types

### Type 1: Mail-Order / Direct Sale
**Mission:** Generate a sale directly from the ad. No salesperson, no showroom, no follow-up brochure. The ad must close the deal.

**Characteristics:**
- LONG copy — must give complete information
- Must answer every buyer question within the ad
- Must lay all fears to rest and overcome every objection
- Must include the full offer: price, guarantee, bonuses, ordering mechanism
- Devotes significant space to the response mechanism (order form, URL, payment options)
- Half-page newspaper ads can run 1,000+ words

**Where used:** Landing pages selling products directly, long-form sales letters, video sales letters, infomercials, direct-response print ads, email campaigns with "buy now" CTAs

**Key question to ask the user:** "Does this ad need to close the sale by itself, or will there be a follow-up step?"

### Type 2: Lead Generation
**Mission:** Get the prospect to raise their hand and request more information. A salesperson, brochure, demo, or follow-up sequence will do the actual selling.

**Characteristics:**
- May be long or short, but NEVER gives the full story — always leaves the reader wanting more
- To get complete information, the reader must respond (submit a form, call, request a brochure)
- CTA is soft: "Download the free guide," "Request a demo," "Get your free consultation"
- Must be compelling enough to generate a response, but not so complete that the reader feels no need to follow up
- Focus on the BENEFIT of responding (what the free resource or consultation will give them), not just the product

**Where used:** B2B ads, lead-gen landing pages, white paper download pages, webinar registration pages, LinkedIn ads, trade magazine ads

**Key insight from the book:** "Most products sold to business and industry cannot be sold directly, but require a salesperson to give a presentation and close the sale in person." Understanding the buying process — and where your ad fits in it — is essential.

### Type 3: Awareness / Brand Building
**Mission:** Generate awareness of the product and a desire to use it over time. Not designed to produce an immediate sale.

**Characteristics:**
- The reader may not need the product right now — the goal is to be top-of-mind when they DO
- Builds awareness and preference over an extended period through repeated exposure
- Often shorter copy, since it's planting seeds rather than closing
- Focuses on brand image, emotional connection, and product personality
- May not include a direct CTA or response mechanism

**Where used:** TV commercials for packaged goods, social media brand campaigns, display/banner ads, sponsorships, billboards, podcast ads

**Example:** "Burger King knows you don't always rush out to buy a Whopper after viewing a Burger King commercial. The goal is to make Burger King the first place you think of when you want a hamburger."

**Caution:** Awareness advertising is expensive and hard to measure. Most small and medium businesses can't afford pure brand building — they need every ad to generate a measurable response. Bly advises: "Don't be afraid to try for immediate action and sales as well as long-range 'image building.' Ask for the order, and ask for it right away."

### Type 4: Corporate / Image Advertising
**Mission:** Sell the company, not a specific product. Create a certain image of the company in the prospect's mind.

**Characteristics:**
- Aimed at a specific corporate audience: stockholders, investors, business community, potential employees, regulators
- May address a misconception, promote the company generally, or establish thought leadership
- Often appears in business publications (Forbes, Fortune, Businessweek)
- CTA is typically implicit (visit our website, learn more about us) rather than a hard sell

**Where used:** Annual reports, company brochure, "About" pages, investor relations, recruitment advertising, corporate social responsibility campaigns, trade show presence

## The Retail Sub-Type: Price and Where to Buy

A specialized format used by retailers. Simple display ads that emphasize:
1. The price of the merchandise (usually a sale or discount)
2. Where to buy it (directing to a physical store location or website)

These ads center around a storewide sale or price-off deal. Minimal body copy. The offer IS the ad.

**Where used:** Newspaper ads, local online ads, email blasts for sales events, flyer/circular inserts

## How to Use This Taxonomy

### Before Writing, Ask:
1. **What type of ad is this?** (Direct sale, lead gen, awareness, corporate, retail?)
2. **Where does it sit in the buying process?** (First touch? Mid-funnel? Closing?)
3. **What is the reader expected to DO after reading?** (Buy? Request info? Remember us? Nothing specific?)
4. **Is there a follow-up step?** (Salesperson? Email sequence? Landing page? Nothing — the ad must close?)

### Then Adjust Your Approach:

| Element | Direct Sale | Lead Gen | Awareness | Corporate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copy length | Long | Medium | Short-medium | Medium |
| Tone | Urgent, persuasive | Informative, inviting | Emotional, memorable | Authoritative, credible |
| Level of detail | Complete | Enough to intrigue | Minimal | Mission/values focused |
| CTA | Hard (buy now, order today) | Soft (download, register, call) | Subtle or none | Implicit (learn more) |
| Proof needed | Extensive (must close alone) | Moderate (enough to justify responding) | Light (brand trust) | Credentials, track record |
| Price mentioned? | Yes, always | Rarely | No | No |
| Guarantee mentioned? | Yes, prominently | Sometimes | No | No |

---

## Common Mistake

Writing lead-gen copy as if it were a direct sale (too long, too much detail, too hard a sell) — or writing direct-sale copy as if it were lead-gen (not enough information to close, expecting a follow-up that doesn't exist). Match the copy strategy to the ad type.
