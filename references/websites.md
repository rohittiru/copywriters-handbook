# Writing Web Sites

## Key Differences: Web vs. Print
- Web readers scan; they rarely read every word. Structure for scanning.
- Sans-serif fonts (Arial, Helvetica) are more readable on screens than serif fonts.
- Topics should be divided across multiple pages, each focused on one topic in 300–400 words max.
- Hyperlinks let you layer detail — keep the main page lean and link to deeper content.
- The web is two-way — build in interaction (chat, forms, comments, social sharing).
- Digital content is easy to update — first drafts don't have to be perfect, but DO update them.
- Use keywords people would search for to find your content or product (SEO).

## Homepage Copy
The homepage headline must immediately communicate: who you are, what you do, and why the visitor should care. Apply the same headline principles as any ad — lead with the strongest benefit.

Structure:
1. **Headline** — clear value proposition with primary benefit
2. **Subhead** — expand on the promise or state the secondary benefit
3. **Brief body copy** — 2–3 short paragraphs establishing credibility and relevance
4. **Clear CTAs** — what should the visitor do next? (Shop, Learn More, Get Started, etc.)
5. **Trust signals** — logos, testimonials, "As seen in," number of customers

## About Page
This is one of the most-visited pages on any site. Don't waste it on a boring corporate bio. Use it to build trust and differentiate:
- Tell the founding story (briefly) with the "why" behind the company
- Establish credentials and authority
- Show the human faces behind the brand
- Include social proof (awards, press, client logos, numbers)
- End with a CTA

## Product/Service Pages
Each page should focus on ONE product or service and follow the motivating sequence:
1. Headline with primary benefit
2. Problem agitation (what pain does this solve?)
3. Solution (how your product solves it)
4. Features/benefits (bullet list)
5. Proof (testimonials, case studies, data)
6. CTA (buy, request demo, get quote)

## Web Writing Rules
- Front-load key information — put the most important content first
- Use descriptive subheads that tell the story even when body copy is skipped
- Keep paragraphs to 1–3 sentences (shorter than print)
- Use bulleted lists for scannable feature/benefit presentations
- Include internal links to related pages for readers who want more depth
- Write meta descriptions as mini-ads (they appear in search results)
- Every page should have a clear CTA — never leave the visitor wondering "what now?"

## SEO Copywriting Basics
- Research keywords your audience actually searches for
- Include primary keyword in the page title, H1, and first paragraph
- Use related keywords naturally throughout the copy — don't force them
- Write for humans first, search engines second
- Longer, more comprehensive content tends to rank better
- Update content periodically — freshness is a ranking signal

## Why NOT to Use a Rotating Banner
Don't use rotating image carousels/sliders on your homepage. They're popular with designers but hurt conversions:
- Users often ignore them (banner blindness)
- They compete with each other for attention
- The message changes before the reader can absorb it
- They slow page load time
- They reduce the impact of your primary headline

Use a single, strong headline and hero section instead.

## Writing E-Commerce Web Sites
The best e-commerce sites make the transaction as easy and painless as possible. Example: Blue Nile (jewelry) — the entire site is designed around shopping, with product links on the homepage, helpful buying guides, interactive tools, close-up photography, and clear diagrams. The mission is crystal clear from the first screen.

E-commerce copywriting rules:
- Product descriptions should lead with the benefit, not the spec
- Include all information needed to make a buying decision on the page itself
- Use high-quality images with zoom capability
- Provide sizing guides, compatibility charts, and comparison tools
- Display reviews prominently
- Make the add-to-cart and checkout process frictionless

## The Problem with Single-Page Web Sites
Single-page sites (all content on one scrolling page) are popular for their visual appeal but have four problems:
1. Poor for SEO — not enough content to target a wide range of keywords
2. 76% of users say the most important factor on a website is ease of finding what they want — multipage sites are better for this
3. Only 10% say design is most important — single-page sites were created for aesthetics, not usability
4. Users prefer consuming content in small chunks — single-page sites present everything in one intimidating scroll

Recommendation: Use multipage sites for most businesses. Reserve single-page for simple brand sites or event pages.

## Writing a ClickFunnel / Sales Funnel Web Site
A sales funnel is a sequence of pages that takes a visitor from stranger to buyer:
1. Traffic page (ad or social post) → 
2. Landing page (opt-in or sale) → 
3. Thank you / upsell page → 
4. Follow-up email sequence

Each page has ONE job and ONE CTA. Don't distract with navigation, sidebars, or multiple offers. The entire funnel is designed to move the visitor forward through a predefined path.
