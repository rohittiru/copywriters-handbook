# Swipe File: Print Advertisements

## The 9 Characteristics of Successful Print Ads (with examples)

### 1. Headline promises benefit, news, or curiosity
- "How to Cut Your Company's Long-Distance Phone Bill 50% or More"
- "For $30 you can acquire the same Assertiveness Skills that are now being taught to Fortune 500 managers for up to $5,000 a day"
- "You Can Make Big Money in Real Estate Sales Right Now"

### 2. Visual illustrates the headline's benefit
- Johnson & Johnson: "Toys That Allow Babies to Master New Skills" — photo shows toddler enjoying the toy
- DuPont Teflon: before/after photos of coated vs. uncoated equipment
- Alfalfa seed brochure: side-by-side fields — scrawny vs. lush (planted with their seeds, 40% yield increase)

### 3. Lead paragraph expands the headline theme
**DuPont:** "THE WORSE YOUR CORROSION PROBLEMS, THE MORE YOU NEED DUPONT TEFLON — In harsh, highly corrosive chemical process environments, fluid handling components lined or made with DuPont TEFLON® resins and films..."

**Century 21:** "YOU CAN MAKE BIG MONEY IN REAL ESTATE SALES RIGHT NOW — Business is booming at CENTURY 21. And so are careers..."

**J&J:** "NOW, FROM JOHNSON & JOHNSON, TOYS THAT ALLOW BABIES TO MASTER NEW SKILLS — Your child is growing bigger. Brighter. More curious and eager to..."

### 4. Layout invites reading
- One central visual
- Headline in large, bold type
- Body copy set underneath headline and visual
- Copy set in black type on white background (reverse type kills readability)
- Short columns (easier to read than long ones)
- Short paragraphs (less than 3 lines)

Avoid: headlines set on a slant, reverse type (white on black) for large blocks, too many competing elements, cluttered layouts.

### 5. Body covers all sales points logically
Follow the motivating sequence even within a print ad.

### 6. Copy provides info needed for next step
Include response mechanism: coupon, URL, phone, QR code, "mention this ad" for retail.

### 7. Copy is interesting
"You cannot bore people into buying your product." — David Ogilvy

### 8. Copy is believable
Use testimonials, demonstrations, specifics. "People mistrust advertising. You must work hard to convince readers that what you say is true. The best way to get people to believe you is to tell the truth."

### 9. Ad asks for action
Every ad must close with a clear CTA. Include: company name, address, phone number. For retail: store hours and locations. For mail order: coupon, order form, or URL.

## Ad Format Examples

**Advertorial (editorial-style):** Designed to look like a magazine article. Uses a strong headline, columns of text, informational tone. Often outperforms traditional ad layouts.

**"Price and where to buy":** Retail standard. Simple display ads emphasizing price and directing to a nearby retail outlet. Centers around a sale or price-off deal.

**Small-space ads:** E-zine ads: 50-100 words of text with hyperlink. Can be as effective as full-page for lead generation. Keep headline, one benefit, and response URL.

## "Dull" Headlines That Sell
"Handling Sulfuric Acid" — boring to a creative director. To a chemical engineer battling corrosion? It implies volumes and makes him read every word.

Lesson: "Dull to whom? Dull to you, the advertiser? Or dull to the reader, our potential customer? The real purpose of an ad is to communicate ideas and information about a product. Too many ads are approved because of their entertainment value. That's a waste of money."
