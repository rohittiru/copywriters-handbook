# Writing TV and Radio Commercials

Broadcast copywriting differs from print: you're writing for the ear, not the eye. The audience can't re-read a sentence or pause to think. Your message must be instantly clear, emotionally engaging, and memorable.

## TV Commercials

**Formats:**
- **Slice of life:** Actors portray a realistic scenario where the product solves a problem
- **Demonstration:** Show the product in action, proving its benefit visually
- **Testimonial:** Real customers speak about their experience
- **Spokesperson/presenter:** A credible figure (celebrity, expert, or company representative) addresses the camera directly
- **Humor:** Can be effective IF the humor reinforces the selling message (not just entertainment)
- **Problem-solution:** Dramatize the problem, then show the product as the fix
- **Before/after:** Visual comparison of life without vs. with the product

**Script Format:**
Two columns — VIDEO (left) describes what the viewer sees; AUDIO (right) describes what the viewer hears (dialogue, voiceover, music, sound effects).

**Writing Rules:**
- The first 3–5 seconds must hook the viewer — open with the most compelling visual or statement
- Mention the product/brand name at least 3 times in a 30-second spot
- Show the product being used, not just talked about
- The visual must carry the story even with the sound off (many viewers mute commercials)
- One main message per commercial — don't try to cram in multiple selling points
- End with a clear CTA: "Visit [website]," "Call now," "Available at [store]"
- Keep language simple, conversational, and natural
- Read the script aloud and time it — a 30-second spot = approximately 60–75 words
- Background music should support, never overpower, the spoken message

**Common Mistakes:**
- Humor that people remember but can't connect to the product
- Background music louder than the voiceover
- Too many selling points crammed into too little time
- Not showing the product or mentioning the brand name enough
- Entertainment that doesn't serve the sale

## Radio Commercials

Radio relies entirely on sound — words, voice, music, and sound effects must paint the picture.

**Formats:**
- **Straight read:** Announcer reads the copy directly (most common)
- **Dialogue:** Two or more voices in conversation
- **Dramatization:** Mini story with actors and sound effects
- **Jingle:** Musical commercial with a catchy, memorable tune
- **Celebrity endorsement:** Famous voice delivers the message

**Writing Rules:**
- Write for the ear: short sentences, simple words, conversational tone
- Repeat the key benefit and the company name/phone/URL multiple times
- Use sound effects sparingly but effectively to create mental images
- Dialogue must sound natural — read it aloud with another person
- A 60-second spot = approximately 150–180 words
- A 30-second spot = approximately 75–90 words
- Spell out phone numbers and URLs slowly and repeat them
- Use mnemonic devices to make contact info memorable (e.g., 1-800-FLOWERS)

## Direct Response TV (DRTV) and Infomercials
- Long-form (28 minutes) or short-form (60–120 seconds)
- Follow the motivating sequence at length: problem → solution → proof → offer → CTA
- "But wait, there's more" structure: stack bonuses to increase perceived value
- Urgency: "Call in the next 10 minutes and receive..."
- Include a strong guarantee to remove purchase risk
- Track response with unique phone numbers or URLs per ad/time slot
