# Swipe File: TV and Radio Commercials

## TV Commercial Examples

### Full Script: Galantine Chicken (30 seconds) — Demonstration Format

| VIDEO | AUDIO |
|---|---|
| MS to CU: Golden brown Galantine chicken on platter. | ANNCR (VO): You're looking at a plump, juicy Galantine chicken. But this is no ordinary chicken. Because we've taken out the bones. |
| MS: Man slicing chicken. One-quarter of meat already sliced on platter. | You can slice right through it... |
| CU: Array of chicken dishes on buffet table. | Prepare any number of delicious chicken dishes, from chicken scampi to chicken salad, quickly and easily, without having to cut around bones. |
| MS: Smiling family eating chicken. | A Galantine chicken costs a bit more than an ordinary chicken. |
| CU: Fully sliced chicken on platter. | But then there's no waste. You get one hundred percent meat. |
| CU: Packaged chicken, showing Galantine name and logo. | So if you have a bone to pick with ordinary chicken, try Galantine instead. At your butcher's and at fine grocers everywhere. |

*Why it works: Demonstrates the product's unique benefit (boneless) visually. Every video frame shows the product. The pun in the close ("bone to pick") reinforces the USP. Product name and where to buy appear at the end. Simple, benefit-driven, no wasted time.*

### Full Script: YOURS Beer for Women (30 seconds) — Slice-of-Life + Wordplay Format

| VIDEO | AUDIO |
|---|---|
| MS: Well-dressed couple in fancy restaurant. Man reaches for bottle of beer. Woman slaps his hand away playfully. | WOMAN: Hey, that's YOURS! |
| MS: Man's face, puzzled, grinning. | MAN: If it's mine, why can't I have it? |
| TS: Woman's finger pointing at label on bottle. | WOMAN: Because YOURS is the beer that's made for women only. |
| MS: Woman pouring beer. | SFX: Beer being poured. ANNCR: YOURS is bubbly, light, and has fewer calories than ordinary beer. |
| MS: Woman finishes pouring, picks up glass. | ANNCR: And it comes in convenient ten-ounce bottles that pour one perfect glass of beer... enough to quench your thirst without filling you up. |
| MS: Man reaching for glass of beer. | MAN: Why can't I have YOURS? |
| MS: Woman pulls glass away, smiling. | WOMAN: 'Cause it's mine... |
| TS: Bottle of YOURS against black background. | (Music and logo) |

*Why it works: The brand name IS the joke — "YOURS" creates natural wordplay between possessive and brand name. Benefits are woven into dialogue naturally (bubbly, light, fewer calories, ten-ounce bottles). The scenario is entertaining BUT every moment serves the sell. Product shown multiple times.*

### Demonstration Format (Described)
**Pool chlorinator:** Woman sits by a pool as voice-over describes crystal-clear water. Suddenly she shoots up through the water — she was actually IN the pool. Commercial was shot underwater, dramatically demonstrating water clarity.

**OxiClean Stain Remover:** Demonstrates the product removing stains in real time. Visual proof catches attention.

**My Pillow:** Company owner and inventor enthusiastically explains and demonstrates the pillow's superiority. Combines spokesperson + demonstration formats.

**Flex Tape:** Metal boat sawed in half, taped together with Flex Tape, taken out on a lake at high speed — no leaks. Dramatic proof of the USP.

## The 12 Types of TV Commercials
1. **Demonstrations** — show the product working
2. **Testimonials** — real customers praise the product (hidden camera for authenticity)
3. **Slice of life** — actors in a realistic scenario where the product solves a problem
4. **Lifestyle** — shows the kind of life you'll lead if you use the product
5. **Spokesperson/presenter** — credible figure addresses camera directly
6. **Animation** — animated characters or scenarios
7. **Jingles/musical** — catchy tune makes the brand memorable
8. **Vignettes** — quick series of related scenes
9. **Humor** — can work IF humor reinforces the selling message
10. **Continuing characters** — recurring characters in a series of ads
11. **Reason-why** — logical argument for buying
12. **Emotion** — taps into feelings (love, fear, nostalgia, pride)

## Word Count Guidelines
- 30-second spot: approximately 60-75 words
- 60-second spot: approximately 150-180 words
- Mention product/brand name at least 3 times in a 30-second spot

## Key Rules
- Background music should SUPPORT, never overpower, spoken message
- Visual must carry the story even with sound off (many viewers mute)
- One main message per commercial
- Show the product being used, not just talked about
- Don't sacrifice the selling message for entertainment value
