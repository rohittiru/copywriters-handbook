# Writing Online Ads

Online ads are constrained by character limits, platform rules, and compliance requirements. Every word must work extremely hard. The ad's sole job is to get the click — the landing page does the selling.

## Google/Search Ads (PPC)
- Headline is everything — include the keyword the user searched for
- Match the ad's promise to the landing page content (message match)
- Use numbers and specifics ("Save 47%" beats "Save Money")
- Include a clear CTA in the description ("Get Your Free Quote Today")
- Test multiple headline variations — small changes create big lifts
- Use ad extensions (sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets) to claim more real estate

## Facebook/Instagram Ads
- The image or video stops the scroll; the copy closes the click
- Lead with the benefit or a curiosity hook in the first line (before "See More")
- Keep primary text under 125 characters for full visibility without truncation
- Use social proof in the ad when possible ("Join 10,000+ marketers who...")
- Test creative (images/video) separately from copy — they're independent variables
- Compliance matters: Facebook rejects ads with specific health/income claims. Use indirect angles (e.g., food-based angles for weight loss instead of "lose 10 lbs")

## Banner/Display Ads
- You have about 1–2 seconds to communicate your message
- Use minimal text — typically a headline + CTA button
- Design for visual hierarchy: image → headline → CTA
- Animate sparingly (subtle motion catches the eye; excessive animation annoys)
- Test conservatively — banners are usually supplemental, not primary traffic drivers
- Retargeting banners (shown to previous site visitors) typically outperform cold traffic banners

## LinkedIn Ads (B2B)
LinkedIn is the strongest social platform for B2B lead generation. Ad formats and when to use each:
- **Text ads** — tight character limits like search ads; benefit-led headline + clear CTA. Same discipline as Facebook text ads: creativity bounded by space, so every word sells
- **Sponsored Content** — a promoted feed post (either boosting an existing page post, or "direct sponsored" content that never appears on your page — useful for A/B testing angles without cluttering the feed)
- **Sponsored Messaging (InMail)** — a personalized message delivered to the prospect's LinkedIn inbox: custom greeting, body text, banner image, CTA button. Write it like a short sales letter, not an ad
- **Dynamic ads** — right-rail ads auto-personalized with the member's own profile data (name, photo, company). Spotlight ads drive to a landing page for conversions; follower ads grow your company page
- Targeting is LinkedIn's edge: write copy that names the job title, industry, or seniority you're targeting — the "select the audience" headline function matters more here than anywhere
- Lead with a professional problem or outcome (pipeline, hiring, cost, career), not consumer-style emotional hooks
- Free webinar / free report offers are the classic LinkedIn lead-gen play — gate valuable content, keep the ad copy about the content's payoff
- Pricing runs PPC, CPM, or cost-per-send (messaging); check LinkedIn Marketing Solutions' current ad specs before writing — limits change

## General Online Ad Principles
- Every ad should lead to a dedicated landing page, not a homepage
- The ad's message and the landing page's headline should match closely
- Test one variable at a time (headline, image, audience, placement)
- Track cost per click AND cost per conversion — cheap clicks that don't convert are worthless
- Comply with platform advertising policies before writing — restrictions vary by category and platform
- "Offer extended" after an expiration deadline is generally perceived as credible and acceptable
