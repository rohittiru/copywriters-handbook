# Writing Landing Pages

A landing page is a dedicated page designed to convert visitors into leads or buyers. Its only job is to get the visitor to take ONE specific action.

## Landing Page Objectives
- Opt-in to an email list
- Download a white paper or lead magnet
- Register for a webinar or demo
- Purchase a product
- Request a consultation or quote

Conversion rate = (people who take action / total visitors) × 100. Rates range from <1% to 50%+ depending on offer, traffic quality, and copy strength.

## 10 Keys to Higher Conversion

**1. Build credibility early.** The first screen must overcome skepticism. Use your logo, credentials, a strong testimonial or two, and a pre-head summarizing your authority. Institutions can display their official seal.

**2. Capture non-buyer emails.** Use a floater (slides onto screen from the side/top — not blocked like pop-ups) offering a free report or e-course in exchange for an email address. This captures visitors who aren't ready to buy yet.

**3. Use lots of testimonials.** Testimonials are your most powerful proof. Ask customers for their own words — don't polish them. Natural, conversational language is more believable than polished puffery. Video testimonials are even stronger — but let the visitor click to play, don't auto-play.

**4. Use lots of bullets.** Format: **Feature** — benefit. Example: "**Quick-release adhesive system** — your vehicle graphics stay clean and don't stick together." Online buyers want to feel they're getting a LOT for their money.

**5. Arouse curiosity in the headline.** Promise a big, compelling benefit or use curiosity to make the reader unable to stop reading. Example: "Become a Property Locator Today — and Make $100,000 a Year in the Greatest Real Estate Career That Only a Few Insiders Know About."

**6. Use a conversational copy style.** A landing page is a letter from one person to another. Even if your product is highly technical, write as a human being to a human being. You cannot sell by boring people to death.

**7. Lead with an emotional hook.** Logical selling works, but emotional selling is much stronger. Correctly assess how the prospect is FEELING about the problem right now, and mirror that feeling back to them. For lead-gen pages, stress the free offer in the headline and lead.

**8. Solve the reader's problem.** After the emotional hook, show how your product (or your free information) solves their specific problem. The quickest route to solving the problem should be taking the action on the page — not surfing your site.

**9. Make it timely and current.** Tie into current events, trends, and challenges. Periodically update copy to reflect current conditions. Outdated references destroy credibility.

**10. Stress the guarantee.** For product sales: money-back guarantee, clearly stated. For lead gen: stress that the offer is FREE with no obligation.

## Landing Page Structure

**No navigation.** The reader's only choice is to respond or leave. No menus, no sidebar links, no distractions. Every element on the page must drive toward the single CTA.

**CTA buttons:** Big, bold, colorful. Also hyperlink keywords/phrases in the copy to the transaction page. Include a toll-free number for phone response. Repeat the CTA at the beginning AND the end of the page.

**Layout:** Uncluttered and easy to follow. Graphics should make copy MORE readable, not less. Avoid designs with poor contrast between text and background.

**Credibility elements:** Logo, credentials, testimonials, trust badges, media mentions, number of customers served.

**Copy structure follows the Motivating Sequence:**
1. Headline — strongest benefit or curiosity hook
2. Subhead — secondary promise or credibility builder
3. Opening paragraph — emotional hook, agitate the problem
4. Solution — position your product as the answer
5. Benefits — bullet list of features/benefits
6. Proof — testimonials, case studies, data, credentials
7. Offer details — what they get, what it costs, guarantee
8. CTA — clear, specific call to action
9. P.S. — restate the main benefit or add urgency

## Driving Traffic to Landing Pages
- Search engine PPC (Google Ads) — cost-per-click based on keyword
- Affiliate marketing — partners promote your product for a commission (15–50%)
- Email marketing — solo emails or ads in others' newsletters
- Social media — organic posts and paid ads
- Banner ads — test conservatively; usually a supplement, not primary
- Viral marketing — add "forward this to a friend" with a compelling offer
