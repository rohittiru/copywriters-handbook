### Full Query Letter Example (Pitching an Article to a Magazine Editor)

Dear Mr. Frank:

Is this letter a waste of paper?

Yes — if it fails to get the desired result.

In business, most letters and memos are written to generate a specific response — close a sale, set up a meeting, get a job interview, make a contact. Many of these letters fail to do their job.

Part of the problem is that business executives and support staff don't know how to write persuasively. The solution is a formula first discovered by advertising copywriters — a formula called AIDA. AIDA stands for Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.

First, the letter gets attention... with a hard-hitting lead paragraph that goes straight to the point, or offers an element of intrigue.

Then, the letter hooks the reader's interest. The hook is often a clear statement of the reader's problems, her needs, her desires.

Next, create desire. You are offering something — a service, a product, an agreement, a contract, a compromise, a consultation. Tell the reader how they will benefit from your offering.

Finally, call for action. Ask for the order, the meeting, the phone call.

I'd like to give you a 1,500-word article on "How to Write Letters That Get Results." The piece will illustrate the AIDA formula with a variety of actual letters and memos from insurance companies, banks, manufacturers, and other organizations.

Regards, Bob Bly

P.S. By way of introduction, I'm an advertising consultant and the author of *Technical Writing: Structure, Standards, and Style* (McGraw-Hill).

*Why it works: The query letter itself demonstrates the AIDA formula it proposes to write about. The opening question grabs attention. The body creates interest by identifying the reader's problem (bad business writing). The article pitch creates desire by promising a specific, useful piece. The close asks for action. The P.S. adds credentials. It's a meta-example — the letter sells itself by being an example of what it's selling.*

## Press Release Headlines: Good vs. Bad
**Bad (no news):** "Ajax Dry Cleaners Provides Top-Quality Cleaning at Reasonable Prices" — no editor will cover this.
**Good (useful info):** "Ajax Dry Cleaners Offers Expert Advice on How to Remove Tough Stains" — editors might reprint the advice as a how-to article. Ajax gets publicity as the source.

## 12 Tips on Press Release Format
1. Clarity and accuracy over fancy letterheads
2. Include both PR firm and client as sources
3. Include contact names and phone numbers
4. Place release date under headline, underlined
5. Headline sums up the story in 2-3 lines max
6. Lead contains who, what, when, where, why, how
7. Include a quotable person for all claims
8. Use inverted pyramid — most important info first
9. Keep it to 1-2 pages
10. Double-space the copy
11. End with ### or -30-
12. Include boilerplate company description

## Key Insight
An editor at a technology magazine received 2,000 press releases per month. Your release must telegraph the news in the first 5 seconds of reading. If the editor chops everything but the lead, the guts of your story must still be there.
